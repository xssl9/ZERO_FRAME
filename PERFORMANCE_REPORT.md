# Performance Report

- Renderer defaults to Compatibility for broad Linux/headless reliability; switch to Forward+ in Godot for desktop release if Vulkan is available. Note that `project.godot` does not pin `rendering_method`, so a desktop run actually comes up in Forward+ — which is what volumetric fog, SSIL and SSR require.
- Rain is two `GPUParticles3D`: 3200 drops in a 30 × 14 × 30 m volume that follows the player, and 320 splashes on a flat emitter at foot level. Both are unshaded alpha billboards with `amount_ratio` driven by `RainSystem.intensity`, so turning the rain down costs nothing extra.
- Rain ambience is one looping `AudioStreamPlayer` on Master, stopped rather than muted when the weather is clear.
- The wet ground is one fullscreen pass, and it is the heaviest shader in the project: per pixel it does nine ripple samples (`ripple_max_radius = 1`) plus up to 43 SSR steps (`ssr_max_travel / ssr_resolution`), on top of a screen and depth copy. Cheap knobs, in order: raise `ssr_resolution`, lower `ssr_max_travel`, then set `ripple_max_radius` to 0. Setting `ssr_max_travel = 0` leaves the puddles matte but keeps the ripples.
- Volumetric fog runs at a 128 × 128 × 128 froxel volume (`project.godot`) with temporal reprojection on. It is the single most expensive addition here; `PhotorealEnvironment.shaft_density = 0.0` disables the shafts without touching anything else, and the `PERFORMANCE` preset switches the whole volume off.
- SDFGI is now on from `HIGH` up (four cascades, six at `ULTRA`) with 64 probe rays, and the fog takes GI injection at full strength, so the fog cost scales with the GI cost. On a GTX-1650-class GPU drop to `PERFORMANCE`, which turns off SDFGI, SSIL, SSR and the fog together. Shadow map is 4096 with Soft High filtering, SSAO/SSIL quality 3 and SSR roughness quality 2 — all of these were at or near their lowest settings before.
- `ULTRA` also enables TAA on the root viewport. MSAA 4× stays on in `project.godot`, so `ULTRA` pays for both; if that is too expensive, drop `anti_aliasing/quality/msaa_3d` to 1 rather than turning TAA off, because SDFGI, SSIL, SSR and the fog are all temporally noisy without it.
- Audio buses: `default_bus_layout.tres` adds one `Weapons` bus with an `AudioEffectHardLimiter` at -1 dBFS. Weapon voices and bullet-impact players target it and fall back to `Master` if the layout is missing, so a broken or absent layout cannot silence the game.
- Movement audio: six run-stride variants and five breath variants are loaded once at spawn and assigned to pooled players per step, so there is no per-step allocation. Breathing only starts after ten seconds of sprinting and re-arms from the clip length, not from `AudioStreamPlayer.playing`.
- Headless smoke test: `scripts/tools/all_maps_smoke_test.gd` covers the dev test grid; re-run it after any level change. Last run on Godot 4.7.2 passed with no parser or runtime errors (player on floor, spawn error 0.0, `AK_74M_WeaponCamera` current).
- `scripts/tools/muzzle_flash_capture.gd` stages a single round and writes a PNG. It needs a real GPU context, so it must run *without* `--headless`; it opens a window for a few seconds.
- The dev test grid is 23 static box bodies plus four pergola posts, sharing three `ShaderMaterial` instances, and nine shadow-casting beam meshes with no collision at all; its draw-call and collision cost is negligible. No enemies are spawned.
- Audio: each weapon holds 8 non-positional `AudioStreamPlayer` shot voices and the player holds 4 footstep voices plus one landing voice. Positional players cannot be used for the viewmodel — the weapon `SubViewport` uses `own_world_3d` and has no 3D audio listener, so an `AudioStreamPlayer3D` inside it is silent.
- Bullet impact nodes expire after 45 seconds and each carries a decal (or quad on Compatibility), two one-shot `GPUParticles3D` and one `AudioStreamPlayer3D`; cap sustained fire in stress tests accordingly.
- Muzzle flash is procedural and allocated once per weapon at load: six unshaded `QuadMesh` instances (three crossed petals, a face-on star, a core and a halo disc) sharing three `ShaderMaterial` instances on `shaders/muzzle_flash.gdshader`, one shadowless `OmniLight3D` inside the weapon viewport, one more `OmniLight3D` parented to the world camera, and two pre-built one-shot `GPUParticles3D` (14 sparks, 10 smoke). Each shot only flips visibility, writes a handful of shader uniforms, sets two light energies and restarts the sparks — no allocation, no tweens. The flame lives 42 ms and the lights 75 ms, so at 650 RPM (92 ms per round) at most one is alive at a time; the smoke puff is rate-limited to one per 220 ms so a burst leaves a drifting cloud instead of resetting every round. The shader uses `blend_premul_alpha`, `depth_draw_never` and `unshaded`, so it is fill-rate only, over a few hundred pixels, plus three octaves of value noise per fragment.
- Recoil and viewmodel kick are two scalar springs integrated in `WeaponBase._process` plus three in `BodycamPhysics`, sub-stepped at 120 Hz; cost is constant and independent of fire rate.
- Weapon animation: one `AnimationPlayer` per weapon with `speed_scale` compression instead of blend trees or additive layers, so a full-auto burst costs the same as idle.
- Architecture uses low-cost box collision, shared procedural material patterns and a compact footprint.
- Recommended target: Medium preset, 1080p, 60 FPS. Imported weapon textures are the dominant VRAM cost; downscale 8K source maps to 2K for lower-end GPUs.
